General Security Concepts

Foundational principles, controls, cryptography, and authentication

12%
CIA Triad & Core Principles ▶
Confidentiality
Ensures information is accessible only to authorized personnel. Methods: encryption, access controls, data masking, physical security, training.
Integrity
Ensures data remains accurate and unaltered unless intentionally modified. Methods: hashing, digital signatures, checksums, access controls, regular audits.
Availability
Ensures systems and resources are accessible when needed. Achieved via redundancy: server, data, network, and power redundancy.
Non-Repudiation
Ensures actions cannot be denied. Digital signatures = hash the message, then encrypt the hash with the sender's private key.
CIANA Pentagon
Extension of CIA Triad — adds Non-repudiation and Authentication.
⚡ Exam Tip: When a question mentions "cannot deny an action" → think Non-Repudiation + Digital Signatures.
Triple A's — Authentication, Authorization, Accounting ▶
Authentication (5 Factors)
  • Something you KNOW — password, PIN (Knowledge Factor)
  • Something you HAVE — smart card, token (Possession Factor)
  • Something you ARE — fingerprint, face (Inherence Factor)
  • Something you DO — keystroke pattern (Action Factor)
  • Somewhere you ARE — GPS, IP location (Location Factor)
Authorization
Permissions granted after authentication. Protects sensitive data, maintains system integrity.
Accounting
Tracks all user activities. Tools: Syslog servers, Network Analysis Tools, SIEM. Creates audit trail, supports forensics, regulatory compliance.
MFA
Requires 2+ factors from different categories. More factors = more secure but less convenient.
Security Control Categories & Types ▶
4 Control Categories
  • Technical — hardware/software (firewalls, encryption)
  • Managerial — strategic planning, governance, policies
  • Operational — day-to-day procedures, human actions
  • Physical — tangible measures (locks, cameras, fences)
6 Control Types
  • Preventive — stop attacks before they happen (firewall)
  • Deterrent — discourage attackers (warning signs)
  • Detective — identify attacks in progress (IDS)
  • Corrective — reduce impact after an attack (backups)
  • Compensating — alternative when standard control unavailable
  • Directive — direct actions (policies, signs)
⚡ Exam Tip: Know the difference between categories AND types — questions often mix them up as distractors.
Zero Trust Model ▶
Principle: "Never trust, always verify" — no user or system is trusted by default, regardless of location.
Control Plane
Adaptive identity, threat scope reduction, policy-driven access control, secured zones.
Data Plane
Subject/system, policy engine, policy administrator, policy enforcement points (PEP).
Cryptography Fundamentals ▶
Symmetric Encryption
Same key for encryption and decryption. Fast. Key distribution is the challenge. Examples: AES, DES, 3DES, RC4.
Asymmetric Encryption
Public key (encrypt) + Private key (decrypt). Slower but solves key distribution. Examples: RSA, ECC, Diffie-Hellman.
Hashing
One-way function, fixed-size output. Verifies integrity. MD5 (128-bit), SHA-1 (160-bit), SHA-256/SHA-3. Salting prevents rainbow table attacks.
PKI (Public Key Infrastructure)
Framework for managing digital certificates and public-key encryption. CA signs certificates, CRL lists revoked certs.
Key Concepts
  • SSL/TLS — secure web traffic (HTTPS = TLS)
  • IPSec — secures IP layer (AH for integrity, ESP for encryption)
  • Steganography — hiding data inside other data (images, audio)
  • Obfuscation — making data difficult to understand
  • Tokenization — replacing sensitive data with a non-sensitive token
⚡ Remember: Digital signatures use ASYMMETRIC encryption — private key to sign, public key to verify.
Access Control Models ▶
ModelHow it works
MACSecurity labels on users AND resources. OS enforces, not the owner. Common in government.
DACResource OWNER decides who can access. Flexible but risky.
RBACPermissions assigned to ROLES; users assigned to roles. Best for enterprises.
Rule-BasedRules/ACLs applied network-wide (firewall rules).
ABACAccess based on attributes: user, resource, environment. Most flexible/granular.
Principle of Least Privilege
Users get only the minimum access needed for their job. Review regularly to prevent authorization creep.
Data Protection ▶
Data States
  • Data at Rest — stored; protect with FDE, partition/file/database encryption
  • Data in Transit — moving; protect with TLS, VPN, IPSec
  • Data in Use — being processed; protect with secure enclaves, app-level encryption
Commercial Classifications
  • Public → Sensitive → Private → Confidential → Critical
Government Classifications
  • Unclassified → Sensitive but Unclassified → Confidential → Secret → Top Secret
Data Ownership Roles
  • Data Owner — senior exec, labels and protects data
  • Data Controller — determines storage/collection purposes
  • Data Processor — hired to collect/process data
  • Data Custodian — manages systems that store data
  • Data Steward — ensures data quality and proper labeling
  • Privacy Officer — oversees PII, PHI, SPI compliance
DLP (Data Loss Prevention)
Strategy to prevent sensitive data from leaving the organization. Can be endpoint-based or network-based.
Identity & Access Management (IAM) ▶
IAM 4 Processes
  • Identification — user claims identity (username)
  • Authentication — verifies identity (credentials)
  • Authorization — grants permissions (RBAC)
  • Accounting/Auditing — tracks activities (logs)
SSO Protocols
  • LDAP — directory protocol, central auth repository
  • OAuth — token-based authorization for 3rd-party apps (uses JWT)
  • SAML — XML-based, redirects to IdP for auth (enterprise federation)
  • OpenID Connect — identity layer on top of OAuth 2.0
PAM (Privileged Access Management)
  • Just-In-Time (JIT) Permissions — grant admin access only when needed
  • Password Vaulting — stores privileged credentials securely
  • Temporal Accounts — time-limited access, auto-disabled
Password Attacks
  • Brute Force — tries every combination
  • Dictionary — uses list of common passwords
  • Password Spraying — few passwords, many accounts (avoids lockout)
  • Hybrid — combines dictionary + brute force (adds numbers/symbols)

Attack types, malware, social engineering, and threat actors

Vulnerability Types & Indicators ▶
Common Vulnerability Types
  • Zero-Day — unknown to vendor, no patch available
  • Race Condition — exploits timing issues in process execution
  • Buffer Overflow — writes more data than a buffer can hold → arbitrary code execution
  • Injection (SQLi, Command, LDAP) — unsanitized input processed as code
  • Improper Error Handling — reveals system info in error messages
  • Misconfiguration — default passwords, open ports, unnecessary services
9 Indicators of Malware Attack
  • Account lockouts, Concurrent sessions, Blocked content
  • Impossible travel, Resource consumption spikes
  • Resource inaccessibility (ransomware), Out-of-cycle logging
  • Missing/cleared logs, Published attack reports
CVE & CVSS
CVE — Common Vulnerabilities and Exposures: unique IDs for known vulnerabilities. CVSS — Common Vulnerability Scoring System: 0–10 severity score.
Application & Code Vulnerabilities ▶
OWASP Top Threats
  • Injection attacks (SQL, Command, LDAP)
  • Broken Authentication / Session Management
  • XSS, CSRF, IDOR
  • Security Misconfigurations
  • Sensitive Data Exposure
Application Security Testing
  • Static Analysis (SAST) — reviews source code without executing
  • Dynamic Analysis (DAST) — tests running application
  • Fuzzing — sends random/malformed data to find crashes
  • Package Monitoring — checks 3rd-party library security
Supply Chain Attacks
Targets less-secure elements in the supply chain (software vendors, hardware manufacturers) to compromise downstream targets. SolarWinds is the classic example.

Security Architecture

Network design, cloud, virtualization, and infrastructure security

Network Security Design ▶
Network Segmentation
Divides network into smaller zones to limit lateral movement. Prevents one compromise from spreading.
DMZ (Demilitarized Zone)
A semi-trusted zone between the internet and internal network. Hosts public-facing services (web servers, email gateways).
VLANs
Logical segmentation at Layer 2. Separates traffic without additional physical hardware.
Network Devices
DeviceFunction
FirewallStateful or stateless packet filtering between network zones
NGFWDeep packet inspection, application awareness, IPS integration
IDSIntrusion Detection System — monitors and alerts (passive)
IPSIntrusion Prevention System — monitors and blocks (inline/active)
WAFWeb Application Firewall — filters HTTP/HTTPS traffic
ProxyIntermediary for client requests; forward vs. reverse proxy
NACNetwork Access Control — enforces policy before granting access
Load BalancerDistributes traffic across multiple servers for availability
SIEMAggregates/analyzes log data; real-time alerting and correlation
Cloud Security ▶
Cloud Service Models
  • IaaS — Infrastructure as a Service (virtual machines, storage) — you manage OS up
  • PaaS — Platform as a Service (dev environments) — provider manages runtime
  • SaaS — Software as a Service (email, CRM) — provider manages everything
Deployment Models
  • Public — shared infrastructure by cloud provider
  • Private — dedicated to one organization
  • Hybrid — combines public and private
  • Community — shared among organizations with similar needs
Shared Responsibility Model
Cloud provider secures the infrastructure (of the cloud); customer secures their data and configurations (in the cloud).
Cloud Security Concerns
  • Data sovereignty — data subject to laws of country where stored
  • Misconfiguration — #1 cause of cloud breaches (open S3 buckets)
  • Insecure APIs — poorly secured cloud management interfaces
  • CASB — Cloud Access Security Broker, enforces policies between users and cloud
Virtualization & Containerization ▶
Hypervisors
  • Type 1 (Bare Metal) — runs directly on hardware (VMware ESXi, Hyper-V)
  • Type 2 (Hosted) — runs on top of OS (VirtualBox, VMware Workstation)
VM Threats
  • VM Escape — breaking out of VM to affect host/other VMs
  • VM Sprawl — unmanaged proliferation of VMs, forgotten machines
  • VM Hopping — attacker moving between VMs on same host
Containers
Lightweight, isolated environments sharing the host OS kernel. Docker/Kubernetes. Faster than VMs. Vulnerable to container breakout attacks.
Serverless / FaaS
Functions as a Service — code runs in stateless, ephemeral containers. Provider manages all infrastructure.

VPN Types
  • Site-to-Site VPN — connects two networks permanently
  • Remote Access VPN — connects individual user to network
  • Split Tunneling — only organization traffic goes through VPN
  • Full Tunnel — all traffic goes through VPN
Business Continuity & Disaster Recovery ▶
Key Metrics
  • RTO (Recovery Time Objective) — max acceptable downtime
  • RPO (Recovery Point Objective) — max acceptable data loss (time)
  • MTTR (Mean Time to Repair) — average time to fix a failure
  • MTBF (Mean Time Between Failures) — average reliability
Site Types
  • Hot Site — fully operational duplicate; ready immediately (most expensive)
  • Warm Site — has hardware, needs data restore (moderate cost/time)
  • Cold Site — has space, needs everything set up (cheapest, slowest)
Backup Types
  • Full Backup — entire data set; slowest to create, fastest to restore
  • Incremental — only changes since last backup; fast backup, slow restore
  • Differential — all changes since last FULL backup; medium speed
  • Snapshot — point-in-time copy, common in VMs
⚡ Exam Tip: RTO = time to recover operations. RPO = how much data you can afford to lose.
Physical Security ▶
Defense in Depth (Physical)
  • Perimeter security — fences, bollards, lighting, guards
  • Building access — badge readers, biometrics, access control vestibules (mantrap)
  • Internal access — locked server rooms, cages, safes
  • CCTV / motion sensors — detection and deterrence
Access Control Vestibule (Mantrap)
Double-door entry system ensuring only one person enters at a time. Prevents tailgating.
Faraday Cage
Shielded enclosure that blocks electromagnetic signals. Prevents wireless eavesdropping and electronic device communication.

Security Operations

Incident response, monitoring, identity management, and vulnerability management

28%
Incident Response Process ▶
NIST IR Phases (PICERL)
  • 1. Preparation — establish IR team, tools, policies, communication plans
  • 2. Identification (Detection) — recognize an incident has occurred
  • 3. Containment — limit spread; short-term vs. long-term containment
  • 4. Eradication — remove root cause (malware, compromised accounts)
  • 5. Recovery — restore systems to normal operation, monitor
  • 6. Lessons Learned — document what happened and improve processes
Incident Categories
  • Event — any observable occurrence in a system
  • Incident — event with negative consequence requiring response
  • Breach — confirmed unauthorized access to data
Forensics Order of Volatility
  • 1. CPU registers/cache → 2. RAM → 3. Network state → 4. Running processes
  • 5. Disk (HDD/SSD) → 6. Logs → 7. Archived media
⚡ Chain of Custody: Must be maintained to ensure evidence is admissible in court. Document every person who handled evidence.
Security Monitoring & Tools ▶
SIEM (Security Information & Event Management)
Aggregates logs from all sources, correlates events, generates real-time alerts. Central hub for security visibility.
SOAR
Security Orchestration, Automation and Response — automates repetitive IR tasks using playbooks.
EDR / XDR
  • EDR — Endpoint Detection and Response; monitors endpoints, detects/responds to threats
  • XDR — Extended DR; integrates data from endpoints, network, cloud
Log Types
  • System logs — OS events, errors, crashes
  • Security logs — auth events, access control
  • Application logs — software events and errors
  • Network logs — traffic, firewall, IDS alerts
  • Audit logs — who did what and when
NetFlow / Packet Capture
NetFlow = metadata about network flows (no payload). Full Packet Capture (pcap) = captures entire packets. Wireshark = pcap analysis tool.
Vulnerability Management ▶
Vulnerability Management Lifecycle
  • 1. Identify — scanning (Nessus, OpenVAS), pen testing, audits
  • 2. Analyze — classify, score (CVSS), prioritize by risk
  • 3. Respond/Remediate — patch, configure, segment, compensate
  • 4. Validate — rescan, audit, penetration test
  • 5. Report — internal/external reporting, responsible disclosure
Scan Result Types
  • True Positive — real vulnerability correctly identified ✓
  • False Positive — not a real vulnerability, but reported as one
  • True Negative — correctly identified absence of vulnerability ✓
  • False Negative — real vulnerability MISSED — most dangerous!
Remediation Options
  • Patching — apply vendor update
  • Network Segmentation — isolate vulnerable systems
  • Compensating Controls — alternative when patching isn't possible
  • Exception — temporary bypass with documented risk acceptance
  • Exemption — permanent waiver (legacy systems)
Threat Intelligence Sources
  • OSINT — Open Source Intelligence (public forums, reports)
  • Proprietary feeds — FireEye, Symantec (paid, more refined)
  • ISACs/ISAOs — industry-specific sharing organizations
  • Dark web monitoring — ahead-of-curve threat intel
Hardening & Configuration Management ▶
System Hardening Steps
  • Remove unnecessary services/software
  • Change default credentials
  • Apply security patches and updates
  • Enable host-based firewalls and IDS
  • Encrypt sensitive data
  • Implement least privilege
Baselines & Benchmarks
CIS Benchmarks and STIG (Security Technical Implementation Guide) provide configuration standards. Baselines define the minimum acceptable security configuration.
Change Management
  • Change Advisory Board (CAB) — reviews and approves changes
  • Scheduled Maintenance Windows — planned downtime for changes
  • Backout Plan — pre-planned rollback strategy
  • Version Control — tracks changes for accountability
Asset Management
  • Hardware inventory — track all physical assets
  • Software inventory — license compliance, patch tracking
  • EOL/EOSL — End of Life systems need special handling or replacement
Network Security Tools & Techniques ▶
ToolPurpose

Penetration Testing Phases
  • Reconnaissance — passive (OSINT) or active (scanning)
  • Scanning & Enumeration — port scans, service discovery
  • Exploitation — gaining access using vulnerabilities
  • Post-Exploitation — privilege escalation, lateral movement
  • Reporting — document findings and recommendations
Pen Test Environments
  • Black Box — tester has no prior knowledge
  • White Box — full knowledge of systems (most thorough)
  • Gray Box — partial knowledge
Red / Blue / Purple Teams
  • Red Team — offensive, simulates attacks
  • Blue Team — defensive, monitors and responds
  • Purple Team — combines red and blue for collaborative improvement
Identity & Access Operations ▶
User Lifecycle
  • Provisioning — creating accounts and assigning rights
  • Deprovisioning — removing access when no longer needed (critical!)
  • Identity Proofing — verifying identity before account creation
  • Attestation — periodically reviewing accounts/access rights
Privilege Issues
  • Privilege Creep — accumulation of excess rights over time
  • Separation of Duties — no single person controls entire process
  • Dual Control — two people required to perform sensitive action
  • Mandatory Vacation — forces coverage, detects fraud
Group Policy Objects (GPO)
Windows mechanism to apply security settings across domain-joined computers. Enforces password policies, software restrictions, firewall rules.

Security Program Management & Oversight

Governance, risk, compliance, data privacy, and security culture

20%
Risk Management ▶
Risk Formula
Risk = Threat × Vulnerability × Impact
Risk Response Strategies
  • Avoid — eliminate the risk by stopping the activity
  • Transfer — shift risk to third party (insurance, contracts)
  • Mitigate — reduce likelihood or impact (controls)
  • Accept — acknowledge and accept the risk (documented)
Quantitative Risk Assessment
  • Asset Value (AV) — dollar value of the asset
  • Exposure Factor (EF) — % of asset lost if threat occurs (0.0–1.0)
  • Single Loss Expectancy (SLE) = AV × EF
  • Annual Rate of Occurrence (ARO) — how often threat expected per year
  • Annual Loss Expectancy (ALE) = SLE × ARO
Qualitative Risk Assessment
Uses descriptive ratings (High/Medium/Low). Risk matrix: Likelihood × Impact. Less precise but faster and easier to communicate.
Governance, Policies & Frameworks ▶
Policy Hierarchy
  • Policies — high-level direction and intent (What)
  • Standards — mandatory rules that support policies (How/What level)
  • Procedures — step-by-step instructions (How to do it)
  • Guidelines — recommended best practices (suggested)
  • Baselines — minimum acceptable security configuration
Key Policy Types
  • AUP (Acceptable Use Policy) — rules for organizational systems
  • BCP (Business Continuity Policy) — ensures ongoing operations
  • DRP (Disaster Recovery Policy) — IT-focused recovery
  • BYOD — rules for personal devices in work environment
  • Data Retention Policy — how long to keep and how to destroy data
Security Frameworks
  • NIST CSF — Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover
  • ISO 27001/27002 — international ISMS standard
  • COBIT — IT governance and management framework
  • CIS Controls — prioritized security best practices
  • ITIL — IT service management best practices

Audits & Assessments ▶
Audit Types
  • Internal Audit — conducted by organization's own team; reviews controls, compliance
  • External Audit — independent 3rd party; required by GDPR, PCI DSS; unbiased
  • Compliance Audit — verifies adherence to specific regulations
Assessment Types
  • Risk Assessment — evaluates likelihood × impact of threats
  • Vulnerability Assessment — identifies technical weaknesses
  • Threat Assessment — evaluates specific threat actors and scenarios
  • Penetration Test — simulated attack (ethical hacking)
Attestation of Findings
Formal, written declaration of audit or assessment results. Provides confirmation and legal documentation.
Third-Party Risk Management
  • SLA (Service Level Agreement) — defines expected service performance
  • MOU (Memorandum of Understanding) — informal agreement on expectations
  • MSA (Master Service Agreement) — overarching contract terms
  • NDA (Non-Disclosure Agreement) — protects confidential information
  • Supply Chain Risk — vendors and partners as attack vectors
Privacy & Data Protection ▶
PII vs. PHI vs. SPI
  • PII — Personally Identifiable Information (name, SSN, address)
  • PHI — Protected Health Information (health records, under HIPAA)
  • SPI — Sensitive Personal Information (financial, biometric, religion)
Privacy by Design
Integrating privacy considerations into system design from the beginning, not as an afterthought. Mandated by GDPR.
Data Destruction Methods
  • Shredding — physical destruction of paper/media
  • Purging — removing data using overwrites or degaussing
  • Sanitization — ensures all data is removed before disposal/reuse
  • Crypto-shredding — destroy encryption keys, making data unrecoverable
Security Awareness & Culture ▶
Security Awareness Training
Regular training is essential. Topics: phishing awareness, password hygiene, social engineering, data handling, incident reporting.
Training Techniques
  • Simulated phishing attacks — test and train employees
  • Computer-Based Training (CBT) — scalable online modules
  • Role-based training — tailored to specific job functions
  • Gamification — engagement through interactive exercises
Culture of Security (4 Phases)
  • Organizational Change Management — exec commitment, shared responsibility
  • Development Phase — create plans, policies, allocate resources
  • Execution Phase — roll out policies, training, adapt to evolving threats
  • Reporting & Monitoring — compliance checks, feedback loops
Insider Threat Indicators
  • Altered state / substance issues, emotional distress
  • Lifestyle incongruences, financial struggles
  • Unusual data access patterns, working odd hours

Exam Strategy & Tips

How to maximize your score on test day

5 Top Score-Boosting Tips ▶
1. Brain Dump First
Use the first 1–2 minutes to write key formulas (SLE, ALE, CVSS, port numbers) on the whiteboard provided. Get them out of your head early.
2. Skip Hard Questions
If a question is taking too long, mark it for review and move on. Students who skip and return score 5–10% higher.
3. Always Guess
No penalty for wrong answers. Eliminate clearly wrong choices first, then pick the best remaining option.
4. Pick Your Best Time
Schedule when you're mentally sharpest. Don't take the exam right after a long workday.
5. Trust Your Preparation
If you've studied, you're ready. Confidence matters. Don't second-guess your first instinct on questions you know well.
Question Strategies ▶
Watch For Distractors
At least 1 of the 4 answers is designed to mislead. Look for answers that sound right but are slightly wrong in context.
Pay Attention to BOLD/ITALICS/CAPS
CompTIA uses formatting to emphasize the exact thing being tested. "Which is MOST appropriate" vs "Which is LEAST" changes everything.
Answer for CompTIA, Not Real World
When in doubt, choose the textbook answer — not necessarily what you'd do in practice. CompTIA answers are based on the highest number of correct situations.
Understand, Don't Memorize
Know WHY the right answer is right. Understanding the concept lets you handle novel scenarios, not just memorized questions.
